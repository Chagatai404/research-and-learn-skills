# Selecting a teaching contract

Choose by the performance sought and the learner's observed attempt. Use the learner's requested depth or medium. The following contracts describe moves and checks; combine them only when the learner needs both. Historical baseline: the concept-tutor revision's TUTOR_PLANNING, LEARNING_PROTOCOL and question contract, and the earlier lecture template's gist → recall → lecture → derivation layers.

Before each diagnostic question identify privately what it **tests**, what it **assumes**, and what it **introduces**. Introduce needed terms and symbols before use unless knowing them is the skill being assessed. A prompt should distinguish a likely gap from a plausible alternative. For “is this a product state?”, an unexplained symbol can hide whether factorization itself is understood. Check notation separately when warranted. Prepare a short criterion, acceptable equivalent reasoning and what assistance would reveal. Choose one assessable action per turn.

| Learner need | Teaching moves | Check of learning |
|---|---|---|
| New term, symbol or category | Define in familiar words, give a minimal example and near non-example, name the distinction | Classify an unfamiliar case and justify the boundary |
| Conflicting intuitions | Surface the learner's prediction, vary one condition, compare predicted and actual behavior | Explain the changed outcome and when the intuition fails |
| Geometric or abstract structure | Map parts of a concrete representation to the formal objects, state where the analogy breaks, then remove the aid | Reconstruct the relation in a different representation |
| Procedural fluency | Demonstrate a small worked example, label each decision and its reason, fade one step at a time | Solve fresh material unaided and catch a plausible erroneous step |
| Derivation or proof | Motivate the target, state assumptions and notation, derive from an established relation, inspect dimensions and a limiting or counterexample case | Reconstruct a consequential step and identify the assumption enabling it |
| Choosing among methods | Compare objectives, information and cost on the same problem, change one assumption | Select and defend a method on a new case |
| Empirical or computational result | Trace inputs, transformations, assumptions, units and output; perturb one choice | Predict the effect and explain which observation would falsify the interpretation |
| Reading a paper | Reconstruct the question, comparison, evidence and boundary from a located passage, figure or table | Explain what narrower claim the evidence warrants |
| Learner's own summary | Obtain raw summary before comparison, quote a specific point, correct omissions without replacing their voice | Revise in their own words, then retrieve later without the comparison |
| Retention or transfer | Begin with free retrieval, vary the structure or setting, keep feedback until after the answer | Distinguish delayed retrieval from same-day retry, transfer from numerical substitution |

Plan general concept → independent example → project application where relevant. Skip dimensions already supported. Evidence about one dimension or one project is insufficient to infer another. On repeated difficulty use a different representation or worked example; revisit with a new independent item after assistance. Teach directly when requested, and resume assessment only when wanted.
